### A Topological Route from Organization to Horizon Formation

**Author:** Kai Stefan Dietrich

# Abstract

The TIG Topological Foundations Program investigates whether physical geometry can be reconstructed from deeper organizational principles.

Rather than assuming geometry, curvature, and horizons as fundamental objects, the program explores the possibility that these structures emerge from admissible organization and connectivity.

The central research route developed in this program is:

Structural Admissibility

↓

Persistent Organization

↓

Connectivity

↓

Emergent Geometry

↓

Emergent Curvature

↓

Critical Transition

↓

Organizational Degeneracy

↓

Branch Formation

↓

Horizon Emergence

↓

TIG Horizon Structure

The objective is not to replace existing physical theories, but to investigate whether a deeper organizational layer may underlie geometric and gravitational phenomena.

---

# Motivation

Most contemporary physical frameworks begin with geometry.

Coordinates, distances, metrics, and curvature are treated as fundamental ingredients.

The TIG Topological Foundations Program investigates an alternative possibility:

> Organization may be more fundamental than geometry.

Under this view, geometry is not assumed.

Geometry emerges.

The program therefore attempts to identify the minimal organizational requirements necessary for the emergence of physical structure.

---

# Structural Admissibility

The program begins with the observation that not every conceivable structure can persist.

A physically meaningful structure must satisfy admissibility conditions.

Admissibility serves as the foundational selection principle.

Only admissible organizational states may participate in subsequent evolution.

---

# Persistent Organization

Admissible states must remain stable under evolution.

Without persistence:

- organization disappears,
- structure collapses,
- physical realization becomes impossible.

Persistent organization therefore represents the first non-trivial requirement of the framework.

---

# Connectivity

Organization alone is insufficient.

Relationships between organizational states must exist.

Connectivity determines:

- accessibility,
- neighborhood structure,
- relational organization.

The framework proposes that connectivity acts as the primary bridge between organization and geometry.

---

# Emergent Geometry

Once connectivity exists:

- neighborhoods emerge,
- relational separation emerges,
- accessibility emerges.

Geometry may therefore be interpreted as a large-scale consequence of organizational connectivity.

The framework investigates the possibility that geometry is derived rather than fundamental.

---

# Emergent Curvature

Variations in connectivity generate variations in geometry.

These variations appear as curvature.

Under this interpretation:

Uniform Connectivity

↓

Flat Geometry

Variable Connectivity

↓

Curved Geometry

Curvature becomes an organizational property rather than a primitive object.

---

# Critical Transitions

As curvature evolves, organizational constraints accumulate.

The system may approach critical organizational states.

Near criticality:

- stability decreases,
- accessibility changes,
- branch structures become possible.

Critical transitions therefore provide the mechanism connecting geometry and horizon formation.

---

# Organizational Degeneracy

A stable organizational system possesses a unique admissible continuation.

Under sufficient constraint accumulation:

multiple admissible continuations may exist.

This condition is called organizational degeneracy.

Degeneracy provides the conceptual origin of branch formation.

---

# Branch Formation

Branches emerge when organizational evolution can no longer proceed uniquely.

One organizational state may permit multiple admissible futures.

Branch structures therefore become natural consequences of organizational degeneracy.

---

# Horizon Emergence

The program proposes that horizons may represent critical accessibility transitions within admissible geometry.

Under this interpretation:

Connectivity

↓

Geometry

↓

Curvature

↓

Critical Transition

↓

Horizon

Horizons become emergent organizational structures.

---

# Relationship to TIG

Within TIG2, horizon formation is described by the cubic condition:

x³ − x² + β³ = 0

The Topological Foundations Program investigates whether this structure may represent the mathematical manifestation of deeper organizational dynamics.

The current program does not derive the cubic law.

It establishes the conceptual route that may eventually enable such a derivation.

---

# Limitations

The current program does not claim:

- a proof of emergent geometry,
- a proof of emergent curvature,
- a proof of organizational degeneracy,
- a derivation of the TIG cubic law,
- a replacement for General Relativity,
- experimental verification.

The framework currently represents a structured research program.

It does not yet represent a completed physical theory.

---

Such a result would establish a complete conceptual route from organization to horizon formation within the TIG framework.
