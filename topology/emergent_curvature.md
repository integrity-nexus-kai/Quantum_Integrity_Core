Core Topological Investigation

Part of the TIG Topological Foundations Program.

## Purpose

Previous investigations established:

↓

↓

Emergent Geometry

A central question remains:

> How does curvature emerge from organizational geometry?

The purpose of this investigation is to explore whether curvature represents a derived property of organizational structure rather than a fundamental ingredient.

---

## Core Research Question

Can curvature emerge naturally from variations in admissible connectivity?

---

## Motivation

Within classical geometry, curvature is typically introduced as a geometric property of space.

Within TIG, an alternative possibility is considered:

Curvature may emerge from variations in organizational structure.

If geometry itself is emergent, curvature should also be emergent.

---

## Organizational Foundation

Assume:

- admissible states,
- connectivity structure,
- emergent neighborhoods,
- emergent distance.

These generate an emergent geometry.

No curvature is assumed.

---

## Uniform Connectivity

Consider a region in which connectivity remains approximately uniform.

In such a region:

- neighborhood growth is stable,
- path structure is regular,
- accessibility remains homogeneous.

This corresponds to a flat organizational geometry.

---

## Connectivity Variation

Now consider a region where connectivity changes.

Possible causes include:

- connectivity concentration,
- connectivity dilution,
- accessibility asymmetry,
- structural constraints.

Neighborhood growth becomes non-uniform.

Accessibility becomes direction dependent.

The geometry ceases to be flat.

---

## Emergence of Curvature

Curvature may therefore be interpreted as:

> the observable consequence of non-uniform organizational connectivity.

Under this interpretation:

Uniform Connectivity

↓

Flat Geometry

while

Variable Connectivity

↓

Curved Geometry

---

## Organizational Interpretation

Curvature does not act as a primary object.

Instead:

Curvature reflects the organizational structure of admissible relationships.

Geometry records the result.

Organization generates the cause.

---

TIG proposes:
