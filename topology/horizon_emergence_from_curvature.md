# Horizon Emergence from Curvature

## Status

Open Foundational Investigation

Part of the TIG Topological Foundations Program.

---

# Purpose

Previous investigations established:

Structural Admissibility
→ Organization
→ Connectivity
→ Metric
→ Dimension
→ Geometry
→ Curvature

A fundamental question remains:

> How do horizons emerge?

The purpose of this investigation is to explore whether horizon formation is an emergent consequence of critical organizational curvature rather than a fundamental geometric boundary.

---

# Core Research Question

Can horizon structures emerge from critical curvature conditions generated by organizational dynamics?

---

# Motivation

Traditional gravitational frameworks typically introduce horizons as solutions of geometric field equations.

The Topological Foundations Program explores an alternative possibility:

> Horizons may emerge when organizational structure reaches critical admissibility conditions.

Under this interpretation, horizons are not primary objects.

They are emergent structural phenomena.

---

# The Horizon Problem

A geometric structure may possess:

- metric organization,
- dimensional organization,
- curvature,

without necessarily producing a horizon.

A horizon therefore requires an additional condition.

The challenge is identifying the organizational threshold at which horizon behavior appears.

---

# Working Hypothesis H1

Horizons are not fundamental geometric objects.

Horizons emerge from critical organizational states.

---

# Working Hypothesis H2

There exists a critical curvature threshold beyond which organizational accessibility changes qualitatively.

---

# Working Hypothesis H3

Horizon formation represents a transition in admissible connectivity structure.

---

# Accessibility Principle

Consider a region of organized geometry.

As curvature increases:

- connectivity patterns change,
- accessibility changes,
- path structure changes.

At a critical threshold, the organizational accessibility of the region may change discontinuously.

This transition becomes a candidate mechanism for horizon emergence.

---

# Connectivity Transition Hypothesis

A horizon may correspond to a transition between:

- globally accessible organization,
- locally trapped organization.

Under this interpretation:

Connectivity Transition
→ Accessibility Transition
→ Horizon

---

# Organizational Criticality

The emergence of a horizon may require:

- critical curvature,
- critical connectivity variation,
- critical organizational compression.

These conditions remain under investigation.

# Horizon as a Structural Boundary

A horizon need not represent a physical surface.

Instead:

> A horizon may represent a boundary between distinct organizational regimes.

This interpretation aligns naturally with admissibility-based approaches.
