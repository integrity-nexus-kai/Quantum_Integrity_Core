## Motivation

The Emergent Metric Principle established a possible route from connectivity to metric structure.

Once a metric exists, a new question arises:

> Why does geometry become curved?

Traditional theories generally treat curvature as a primary geometric quantity.

The present framework investigates whether curvature is instead an emergent organizational phenomenon.

---

## Uniform Connectivity

Consider a region in which connectivity remains approximately uniform.

In such a region:

- neighborhood growth remains stable,
- path structure remains regular,
- accessibility remains homogeneous,
- metric behavior remains approximately constant.

The resulting geometry appears flat.

---

## Connectivity Variation

Now consider a region where connectivity varies.

Possible variations include:

- increased connectivity density,
- reduced connectivity density,
- asymmetric accessibility,
- constrained neighborhood growth.

These variations alter path structure.

Metric behavior becomes non-uniform.

## Curvature as Connectivity Gradient

A working interpretation is:

> Curvature reflects variations in connectivity organization.

Flat regions correspond to relatively uniform connectivity.

Curved regions correspond to non-uniform connectivity.

Curvature therefore becomes an observable signature of organizational variation.
