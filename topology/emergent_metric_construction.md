# Emergent Metric Construction

## Status

Open Foundational Investigation

Part of the TIG Topological Foundations Program.

---

# Purpose

Previous investigations established the following structural sequence:

Admissibility
→ Organization
→ Connectivity
→ Geometry

A central step remains unresolved:

> How does connectivity become a metric?

The purpose of this investigation is to explore whether metric structure can emerge from admissible connectivity relations rather than being assumed a priori.

---

# Core Research Question

Can a metric arise as a consequence of connectivity structure?

---

# Motivation

Modern geometry typically begins with a metric.

Distances are assumed.

Lengths are assumed.

Space-time intervals are assumed.

The Topological Foundations Program explores the reverse possibility:

Connectivity
→ Distance
→ Metric
→ Geometry

Under this view, metric structure becomes an emergent organizational property.

---

# The Metric Problem

A connectivity graph defines:

- states,
- neighborhood relations,
- paths,
- connectivity patterns.

However, a graph does not automatically define:

- length,
- distance,
- curvature,
- geometry.

Additional structure is required.

The challenge is identifying the minimal admissible construction.

---

# Working Hypothesis H1

Distance is not fundamental.

Distance emerges from organizational separation.

---

# Working Hypothesis H2

The shortest admissible path between two states defines an effective distance.

---

# Working Hypothesis H3

Metric structure is a large-scale interpretation of connectivity relationships.

---

# Organizational Distance

Consider two admissible states:

A

B

If A and B are directly connected:

Distance(A,B) = 1

If an intermediate state exists:

A → C → B

Distance(A,B) = 2

Distance therefore becomes a property of connectivity rather than space.

---

# Path-Based Metric Hypothesis

A preliminary metric candidate may be defined by:

- minimal path length,
- transition count,
- transition complexity.

Under this interpretation:

Metric = Minimal Organizational Separation

---

# Connectivity Cost

Not all transitions may be equivalent.

Some transitions may require:

- greater structural change,
- lower admissibility,
- higher organizational effort.

This motivates the concept of connectivity cost.

---

# Weighted Connectivity

Connectivity may therefore be represented by:

State A
→ cost c₁
→ State B

Different paths may possess different effective lengths.

Distance becomes a weighted organizational quantity.
