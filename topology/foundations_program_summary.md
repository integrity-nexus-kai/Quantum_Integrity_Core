# Purpose

The TIG Topological Foundations Program investigates whether physical geometry can be reconstructed from deeper organizational principles.

The program proceeds through a sequence of progressively constrained investigations.

The objective is not to assume geometry, curvature, or horizons.

The objective is to determine whether these structures emerge necessarily from admissible organization.

# Structural Admissibility

The program begins with the principle that not every structure is physically admissible.

Admissibility determines which organizational states may exist.

This defines the foundation of the framework.

---

# Persistent Organization

Admissible structures must remain stable under evolution.

Persistent organization therefore becomes the first non-trivial requirement.

Without persistence:

- structure disappears,
- organization collapses,
- physical realization becomes impossible.

---

# Connectivity

Persistent organization alone is insufficient.

Relationships between organizational states must exist.

Connectivity therefore becomes the first mechanism capable of generating relational structure.

---

# Emergent Geometry

Once connectivity exists:

- neighborhoods emerge,
- accessibility emerges,
- distance emerges.

Geometry appears as a consequence of organizational structure.

Geometry is therefore treated as emergent rather than fundamental.

---

# Emergent Curvature

Variations in connectivity generate variations in geometry.

These variations appear as curvature.

Curvature therefore becomes an organizational property rather than a primitive object.

---

# Critical Transitions

Curvature evolution may generate critical organizational states.

At critical thresholds:

- accessibility changes,
- stability changes,
- branch structures emerge.

Criticality becomes the mechanism connecting geometry and horizons.

---

# Horizon Emergence

Under this interpretation:

# Central Working Hypothesis

The central hypothesis of the TIG Topological Foundations Program is:

> Physical horizon structures emerge from admissible organizational dynamics.

This hypothesis motivates the entire TIG research architecture.

---

# Open Questions

Several important questions remain unresolved:

- Can admissibility be formalized uniquely?
- Can connectivity be reconstructed mathematically?
- Can curvature be derived directly from organization?
- Can criticality be reconstructed from connectivity variation?
- Can the TIG cubic structure be derived from organizational principles?

These questions define the next stage of TIG development.

---

# Success Criterion

The TIG Topological Foundations Program succeeds if:

> horizon formation can be reconstructed from admissible organizational principles without assuming horizons as fundamental objects.

Such a result would establish a complete conceptual route from organization to horizon formation within the TIG framework.
