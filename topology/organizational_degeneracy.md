---

## Core Research Question

Can branch formation be derived from organizational degeneracy?

---

## Motivation

A stable organizational system possesses a unique admissible continuation.

As long as admissibility conditions remain sufficiently unconstrained:

One State

↓

One Future

The existence of branches implies that this uniqueness has been lost.

The mechanism responsible for this loss is called organizational degeneracy.

---

## Definition

Organizational degeneracy occurs when multiple distinct organizational continuations satisfy the same admissibility requirements.

Formally:

One Organizational State

↓

Multiple Admissible Continuations

No unique continuation exists.

---

## The Uniqueness Principle

A non-degenerate organizational system possesses:

- a unique continuation,
- stable accessibility,
- predictable evolution.

Organizational evolution remains deterministic with respect to admissibility.

---

## The Constraint Accumulation Principle

As organizational complexity increases:

- relationships increase,
- connectivity increases,
- accessibility constraints increase,
- admissibility requirements become more difficult to satisfy simultaneously.

The system accumulates structural tension.

---

## Critical Constraint Regime

At sufficiently high organizational tension:

multiple organizational continuations may satisfy admissibility equally well.

The system can no longer select a unique continuation.

Organizational degeneracy emerges.

---

## Degeneracy Transition

The transition can be represented conceptually as:

Unique Evolution

↓

Constraint Accumulation

↓

Organizational Degeneracy

↓

Multiple Admissible Futures

This represents the first stage of branch formation.

---

## Accessibility Interpretation

Before degeneracy:

all admissible states remain connected through a common accessibility structure.

After degeneracy:

multiple accessibility sectors may exist.

The organizational landscape separates into distinct admissible regions.

---

## Relationship to Branch Structure

Branches do not appear spontaneously.

Branches emerge when degeneracy becomes unavoidable.

Under this interpretation:

Organizational Degeneracy

↓

Branch Emergence

Degeneracy therefore acts as the precursor to branching.

---

## Relationship to Criticality

Degeneracy naturally generates critical behavior.

Near a degenerate state:

- stability weakens,
- sensitivity increases,
- accessibility changes,
- branch structures become possible.

Criticality therefore emerges from degeneracy.
