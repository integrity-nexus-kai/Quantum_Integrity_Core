# Minimal Structure Comparison

## Status

Open Foundational Investigation

Part of the TIG Topological Foundations Program.

---

# Purpose

The Volumetric Organization Necessity investigation suggests that admissible physical organization may require a volumetric structural framework.

The next question becomes:

> Which volumetric structure provides the most efficient foundation for persistent admissible states?

The purpose of this investigation is to compare candidate structures using a common set of admissibility criteria.

---

# Core Research Question

Which volumetric structure maximizes:

- persistence,
- integrity,
- connectivity,
- addressability,
- recursive growth,

while minimizing organizational complexity?

---

# Selection Criteria

All candidate structures are evaluated according to:

## C1 — Persistence

Can the structure remain stable under local perturbations?

---

## C2 — Integrity

Can local failures be absorbed without immediate global collapse?

---

## C3 — Connectivity

Does the structure support stable neighbor relationships?

---

## C4 — Addressability

Can individual states be uniquely identified and referenced?

---

## C5 — Recursive Growth

Can the structure expand while preserving organization?

---

## C6 — Organizational Cost

How much complexity is required to maintain the structure?

---

# Candidate Structures

## Point Structure

### Advantages

- Minimal definition

### Limitations

- No neighborhood structure
- No volumetric organization
- No recursive spatial organization

### Assessment

Insufficient.

---

## Linear Structure

### Advantages

- Simple connectivity

### Limitations

- Extremely limited neighborhood relations
- Poor fault tolerance
- Restricted growth behavior

### Assessment

Insufficient.

---

## Planar Structures

### Advantages

- Efficient local organization

### Limitations

- Limited volumetric capacity
- Reduced neighborhood richness

### Assessment

Potentially useful but incomplete.

---

## Tetrahedral Structures

### Advantages

- Strong local rigidity
- High geometric stability

### Limitations

- Complex large-scale organization
- Increased coordination requirements

### Assessment

Strong candidate for local stability.

---

## Octahedral Structures

### Advantages

- High symmetry

### Limitations

- Complex recursive organization
- Higher coordination cost

### Assessment

Requires further investigation.

---

## Hexagonal Structures

### Advantages

- Excellent planar efficiency

### Limitations

- Non-trivial volumetric extension

### Assessment

Strong in two-dimensional organization.

---

## Cubic Structures

### Advantages

- Exact volumetric tiling
- Simple neighborhood organization
- Direct addressability
- Efficient recursive extension
- Minimal organizational overhead

### Limitations

- Requires formal proof of optimality

### Assessment

Primary candidate.

---

# Working Hypothesis H1

The preferred structure is not necessarily the most stable local structure.

The preferred structure is the structure that minimizes the total cost of maintaining persistent admissible organization.

---

# Working Hypothesis H2

Global organizational efficiency may be more important than local geometric rigidity.

---

# Working Hypothesis H3

The optimal structure may emerge from a balance between:

- stability,
- connectivity,
- recursion,
- and organizational simplicity.

---

# Preliminary Observation

Among currently considered candidates, cubic structures appear uniquely effective at combining:

- volumetric organization,
- recursive growth,
- addressability,
- and low organizational cost.

This observation remains provisional.
