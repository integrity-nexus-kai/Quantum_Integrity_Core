# Minimal Persistent Structure Problem (MPSP)

## Status

Open Foundational Research Problem

Part of the TIG Topological Foundations Program.

---

# Motivation

The Structural Admissibility Framework establishes that physical reality cannot be identified solely with mathematical possibility.

Admissible reality requires:

- consistency,
- integrity,
- persistence,
- and stable state organization.

This immediately raises a deeper question:

> What is the minimal geometric structure capable of supporting persistent admissible states?

---

# Core Research Question

Which minimal geometric structure can maintain persistent admissible states under recursive evolution?

---

# Required Properties

## P1 — Persistence

The structure must remain stable under local perturbations.

## P2 — Integrity

Local failure must not necessarily imply global collapse.

## P3 — Admissibility

The structure must support valid state configurations.

## P4 — Connectivity

Stable neighbor relationships must be possible.

## P5 — Addressability

Individual states must be distinguishable.

## P6 — Growth

The structure must permit extension without loss of coherence.

## P7 — Recursion

The structure must support self-similar organization.

# Selection Principle

The preferred structure is the structure that simultaneously maximizes:

- persistence,
- integrity,
- connectivity,
- recursion,
- addressability,
- and growth,

while minimizing structural cost.

---

# Working Hypotheses

H1: Persistent physical organization imposes non-trivial geometric constraints.

H2: The minimal persistent structure is not arbitrary.

H3: Admissibility requirements may uniquely restrict the set of viable geometric organizations.
