# Emergent Dimension Construction

## Status

Open Foundational Investigation

Part of the TIG Topological Foundations Program.

---

# Purpose

Previous investigations established the following structural chain:

Admissibility
→ Organization
→ Connectivity
→ Metric

A fundamental question remains:

> How does dimensionality emerge?

The purpose of this investigation is to explore whether dimension is an emergent consequence of admissible connectivity and metric structure rather than a fundamental assumption.

---

# Core Research Question

Can dimension arise from organizational and metric constraints?

---

# Motivation

Conventional geometry assumes dimension from the beginning.

Space is declared to be:

- one-dimensional,
- two-dimensional,
- three-dimensional,
- or higher-dimensional.

The Topological Foundations Program explores the reverse possibility:

Connectivity
→ Metric
→ Dimension

Under this view, dimension becomes an observable property of organization.

---

# The Dimension Problem

A metric structure alone does not automatically determine dimensionality.

A network may possess:

- local neighborhoods,
- distance relations,
- connectivity structure,

without an obvious dimension.

The challenge is identifying the mechanism by which dimension emerges.

---

# Working Hypothesis H1

Dimension is not fundamental.

Dimension emerges from connectivity organization.

---

# Working Hypothesis H2

Dimension reflects the number of independent organizational directions available to a system.

---

# Working Hypothesis H3

Observed dimensionality is a large-scale property of recursive organization.

# Neighborhood Expansion

Dimension may be inferred from how neighborhoods grow.

If the number of accessible states expands according to a characteristic pattern, effective dimensionality may emerge.

Examples:

- linear growth,
- planar growth,
- volumetric growth.

Growth Pattern
